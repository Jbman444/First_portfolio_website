## Overview
This research investigates how biofilm growth alters groundwater flow and contaminant transport at the pore scale. Biofilms—microbial communities that develop on grain surfaces—can significantly modify pore geometry, changing flow pathways and redistributing velocities within porous media.

These changes directly impact mass transport and reaction rates, with important implications for processes such as groundwater remediation, nutrient cycling, and contaminant degradation. By resolving these effects at the pore scale, this work aims to improve how subsurface systems are represented in larger-scale groundwater models.

## Research Focus
The central question of this work is:

**How does biofilm growth modify flow distribution and, in turn, influence transport and reaction processes in porous media?**

To address this, the project combines experimental imaging with computational modeling to capture both the structure and dynamics of biofilm-impacted systems.

---

## Methods

- Designed a robust workflow for generating pore-scale **Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD)** simulations in porous media  
- Converted high-resolution experimental biofilm images into **3D geometries** using Python and Blender  
- Performed **image segmentation and preprocessing** to isolate pore space and biofilm regions  
- Simulated flow and transport using **OpenFOAM**, resolving velocity fields and concentration dynamics  
- Automated simulation pipelines using **Python and Bash**, improving reproducibility and efficiency  
- Processed large datasets into **publication-quality visualizations**

## Results & Insights

- Biofilm growth leads to **preferential flow channeling**, concentrating flow into limited pathways  
- Reduced pore space connectivity results in **localized velocity increases** and stagnant zones  
- These changes significantly impact **solute residence time and mixing behavior**  
- Transport dynamics suggest strong implications for **reaction rates and contaminant degradation efficiency**  
- Demonstrated that small-scale biological processes can have **large-scale impacts on groundwater system behavior**

## Broader Impact

This work contributes to a better understanding of how biological processes influence groundwater systems. By linking pore-scale dynamics to transport and reaction behavior, the research supports improved modeling of:

- Groundwater remediation strategies  
- Contaminant fate and transport  
- Biogeochemical reaction processes  
- Subsurface flow heterogeneity
